The Grin Reaper

Emilia stared up at the ceiling, bored out of her head. Her eyes flitted around the room, looking for something, anything that would be remotely interesting enough to catch her attention. The result: absolutely nothing. 

Her stomach rumbled, but she was too lazy to get up to eat. It was only after her older sister, Esther, called her downstairs to eat lunch that she stumbled her way down the stairs. 

Esther placed the bowl of steaming hot food in front of Emilia. “Whoa. Who died?” 

Emilia glared at her sister, smoothing out her bed-head and plopping into her seat to eat her lunch. Once she finished eating, she was once again faced with her sister’s preaching. “I know that you have a week off from school, but it doesn’t mean you can get up at this hour.” 

Emilia rolled her eyes and was about to retort when her younger brother stormed into the room, screaming, “The Grim Reaper is coming!” He then proceeded to run around the house, repeatedly shouting. 

Emilia supported her head with her hand. “Ethan, it’s too early in the morning for you to be running around the house screaming.” 

Ethan turned to look at his older sister. There were question marks popping out of his head when he responded, “It’s 1:00, you know.” 

“It’s always the morning when you get up.” 

“Even in the middle of the night?” 

“Yup!” 

Esther face-palmed, listening to their nonsensical conversation. “Both of you, don’t you have homework to be working on?” 

“Ehh … ” both Emilia and Ethan groaned. “No thanks.” 

“Do not procrastinate on your work. You only have this week off. ‘Start earlier and 
end earlier,’ is what I say.” 

“‘Today's only the first day of break,’ is what I say. So we get to rest, right?” Emilia blinked her long eyelashes at her older sister. 

Esther glared at the two, as if she could stare the two into submission. “Today’s a rest day!” Emilia announced, quite clearly opting to ignore the message in Esther’s eyes. 

“Yay!” Ethan also yelled, evidently choosing to also ignore his eldest sibling in favor of Emilia. 

Esther sighed and massaged her temples. “Fine. You can rest today. But make sure to do your homework tomorrow.” 

“Okay,” Emilia and Ethan both replied, though whether or not they were listening was something only they would know. 

Later that night, there was a news segment playing on TV, about how another person had fallen prey to COVID-19. 

“Look! The Grim Reaper has taken another victim!” Emilia yelped. 

“Take cover!” Ethan screamed along with her. Both kids hid under the dining table. Esther lifted up the table cloth with an exasperated look. 

“Stop playing around and eat. I called you guys 10 minutes ago.”

“But the Grim Reaper will get us!” Emilia protested. 

Esther could feel that her patience was not far from snapping. “Stop playing around.” 

“But … but … the Grim Reaper …” 

“Eat! Now!” 

“Yessir, Ma’am sir!” 

Esther glared at her siblings who were completely unrepentant. “And do your homework after dinner! No ifs, ands, or buts!” 

“But today’s a break day!” Emilia protested. 

“That’s a but,” Ethan pointed out. 

Emilia glared at her less-than-helpful youngest sibling. 

“Ethan’s right,” Esther blabbered on. “Even if today was a rest day, you have rested for most of the day. As a wise person once said: ‘you must have a balance between work and play.’ Because you’ve played for most of the day, you must now work to balance it out.” 

“Translation?” Emilia asked, not wanting to listen to Esther’s long-winded speech. 
Esther muttered something under her breath: something having to do with no one being respected in this household and so on, before repeating his previous words in simpler terms: “I will not condone procrastination.” 

“More like, you won’t condemn it.” 

Esther’s patience snapped. “Go eat!” 

“Yessir, Ma’am sir!” Emilia repeated, completely unrepentant. 

Esther growled something unintelligible as he watched her younger siblings grab their plates. “Wash your hands!” she barked out. 

Later, in the dead of night, Esther felt a chill run down her back. “Did I set the air conditioning too low?” she wondered.

The next day. 

“Wake up.” Esther shook her little sister awake. 

Emilia opened her bleary eyes. “What time is it?” 

“8:00,” was the response. 

Emilia yawned. “ … That so? Good night.” 

Esther pulled Emilia’s blankets off. “Wake up,” she repeated, a little more force in her voice. 

“But it’s only 8:00,” Emilia complained. 

“You mean ‘it’s already 8:00’,” Esther corrected. “‘Early to rise and early to bed,’ is what I say.” 

“Are you a monk?” Emilia muttered. 

Esther glared at her. “I heard that. Come on, out of bed now. You have to do your homework today.” 

“Ehhh … ” 

“Let’s see. Try to do homework from two classes today, okay?” 

“Ugh.” Emilia groaned and attempted to crawl back into bed, easily being stopped by her sister. 

“Come on; the earlier you start, the earlier you finish.” 

Emilia continued to grumble and groan as she got ready, but Esther eventually managed to get her up-and-running. 

“So which subjects are you going to tackle today?” Esther asked. 

“Art and theater.” 

“You were taking a theater class?” 

Emilia smiled sheepishly. “No. It’s club work. We have to make props for our next performance.” 

Esther raised an eyebrow. “I will do real work tomorrow,” Emilia promised. 

Esther knew she didn’t have to supervise her little sister when she was doing work she liked so Esther simply got started on her own homework.

“Are you done with your goals for today?” Esther asked as the three sat down for dinner. 

“Yup!” Emilia beamed. “All done!” 

“See? You can do it if you try!” As Esther went back to the kitchen to grab utensils, she missed her younger siblings cackling. 

That night, as she got ready for bed, Esther heard the floorboards creaking. She dismissed the sound as one of her siblings going to get a drink of water. However, she heard the sound again and again. She eventually got out of bed, ready to lecture whichever sibling of hers was quite clearly not sleeping. 

As soon as she opened his door, she saw a shadow on the wall. She attempted to discern the blurry figure when all of a sudden … it cleared! 

The figure, illuminated by the lampposts outside, was a shadowy figure, wearing a cloak. It held a glistening scythe that reflected the moon’s light, dripping with some unknown liquid. 

“Aah!” Esther screamed. She immediately darted back into his room, her heart racing faster than a racehorse. She stayed in his room until she managed to calm down. When she opened her door to peek outside, the hallway was bare. Was it just a hallucination? she wondered. 

By the time the sun rose, Esther hadn’t slept a wink, portrayed by the dark, black bags under her eyes. She freaked both Ethan and Emilia out when she woke them up, with Emilia asking her “Who died?” 

The third night of vacation, it was raining. Esther was once again woken up by footsteps, barely audible over the roaring thunder and flashes of lightning. 
The fourth night, Esther left the lights on. She decided to wait for the so-called Grim Reaper to show up. Just as the clock struck 12:00, the lights … turned off! 

For a moment, Esther was shocked. But then, she remembered that her parents had set the lights to automatically shut off at midnight. She attempted to turn the lights back on, but they only stayed on for a second before flickering out. 

“Huh?” Esther was perplexed. She repeatedly pressed the light switch but the lights refused to turn on. “Maybe a power outage?” she wondered. 

It was only until Esther was too tired to wait for the Grim Reaper that she went to bed. As she tucked herself in, the lights suddenly turned on. “Now it turns on?!” Esther huffed. As she got up to switch the lights off, the lights flickered out again. 

Esther furrowed her eyebrows as the lights continuously switched on and off, on and off, in a never-ending stream. 

“Who’s there?!” Esther called out. 

Shing! The sound of a metal blade echoed throughout the room. “Show yourself!” Esther demanded, her legs beginning to tremble. 

“Wa-ha-ha-ha-ha!” an evil laugh permeated the room and the silhouette of the figure Esther had seen on the second night appeared on the curtains. 

“Everyone has a time, and yours has come!” the voice announced. Esther’s eyes started to tear up from the fear of the booming and screechy voice. Finally, she managed to calm himself enough to speak a coherent sentence. “Just … just take me, okay? Leave my little siblings out of this.” 

At her words, everything … stilled. The lights stayed on, the scythe sounds stopped, and the silhouette disappeared. Even the howling winds from the tropical storm seemed to have subsided. 

As Esther was calming down her racing heart, she heard a knock on the door. “Esther?” 

Esther recognized the voice as Emilia’s. She wiped his tears away and opened the door. “What’s wro—”

Her eyes landed on the Grim Reaper cutouts and dolls that Emilia held, as well as the voice changer in Ethan’s. Esther’s eyes widened in realization as her younger siblings smiled sheepishly. “Umm … sorry?” 

Esther’s legs collapsed as the tension left her body. “Thank goodness,” she whispered in relief. 

Emilia and Ethan both set down their items and hugged their older sister. “We’re sorry for pranking you!” 

As Esther listened on, Emilia and Ethan explained the matter in its entirety. “It was my idea,” Emilia confessed. “I didn’t want to do homework, so Ethan and I came up with the idea that if you stayed up, you would lose sleep, and wouldn’t have the energy to lecture us.” 

Esther sighed. “Shouldn’t you try to put those brain cells into actually doing your work? Instead of trying to get out of doing your homework, it would just be easier if you were to spend your time working on it. In the end, whether I police you to do it or not, it still has to be done. Why not complete it earlier so you don’t have to worry about it for the rest of the break?” 

Emilia hung her head. 

“And you, Ethan,” Esther turned to her youngest sibling. 

“I’m done with all my work!” Ethan yelped before Esther could continue. 

“Even if you are done, you shouldn’t be distracting Emilia from her work.” 

“Okay … ” Ethan whispered. 

“Now, it’s time to go to sleep.” 

Emilia and Ethan groaned. “But we’re too awake to go to sleep!” 

Esther glared at the two, but her puffy red eyes lessened the effect. 

“Fine … ” the two muttered. 

Esther watched the two walk away, their backs slouching. She called after them, “We can prank Mom and Dad with this later, okay?” 

Emilia and Ethan perked up at that. “Okay!” they scampered away to their respective rooms. 

Later that week, when their parents came home, they got quite a fright, but that’s a story for another time. 

The vacation quickly passed by. 

Emilia sighed. “Vacations always go so quickly,” she complained. 

Esther rolled her eyes. “It’s because time flies when you’re having fun.” 

Emilia glanced at her. “Another one of your ‘words of wisdom?’” 

Esther smirked. “You know it. Now, wear your mask properly. You don’t want to get the Grim Reaper to get you!” 

The End.

Closure

Janet smiled as she opened the front door. “Matthew! Long time no see!” 

Matthew smiled. “It’s been a while, Janet. How’re you doing?” 

“Great!” Janet stepped aside to let Matthew in. “But you haven’t been coming over recently. It’s lonely.” 

Matthew laughed. “Sorry, Janet. I got busy with graduation and working, so I had less free time. Plus, it seemed like your parents were doing better after Justin ― ” 

Matthew stopped himself as he saw Janet’s eyes dim. “Sorry, you don’t like me mentioning your brother, right?” 

Janet shook her head. “It’s fine. You were his friend after all. Anyways, how’ve you been doing?” 

“Managing. Being in the workforce is very different from being in school. How’re your parents doing?” 

At the mention of her parents, Janet’s smile, which she had regained again with much effort, faded visibly. “ … Fine,” she managed to spit out. “Same old, same old.” 

Matthew noticed her chilly tone. “Just remember they’re going through a lot as well.” 

“Sure.” 

Matthew didn’t know how much of that Janet really meant, and he just shook his head helplessly as he followed Janet into the house. Inside the house, the family of three, plus Matthew, sat down for dinner. The conversation was pretty lively, as everyone caught up on each other’s lives. Janet picked at her food, catching the attention of her mother. 

“What’s wrong, honey? Is the food not to your liking?” 

“It’s fine, Mom.” 

“Oh. Do you need something to drink? I’ll get you anything you want.” 

“I’m not thirsty.” 

“I-is that so?” 

Janet’s mom quietly chewed her food, casting glances at her daughter. She cleared her throat. “You know, your brother ― “ 

Janet slammed her hands down on the table, rattling the silverware and causing a cup to tip over. “Brother, brother, brother! That’s all you guys talk about! What about the people who are still alive?! What about the person in front of you?! What about me?! Do you really think I want to live my life being surrounded by how much you guys miss my brother?!” Janet stormed up the stairs, leaving three frozen people in the dining room. 

Once she had recovered, Janet’s mom had tears streaming down her face. “Why can we never get this right?” 

Matthew excused himself and chased after Janet. He knocked gently on her door. “Can I come in?” 

Janet sniffled. “Yeah.” 

Matthew quietly opened the door. Janet was sitting on her bed, wiping her red eyes. Matthew sighed softly. “Just hear me out before you say anything.” 

Janet looked at him, a clear sign to continue. 

“Go to the attic. There should be some boxes that say ‘Diary’ on them. Go and read them.” 

Janet stared at him in confusion, but Matthew didn’t explain himself. Janet slowly walked out into the hallway and pulled down the ladder. Coughing, she climbed up to the attic. She found the box that Matthew had told her about and opened it. She took out one of the musty notebooks and blew the dust off of it. “‘Diary XXX,’” she read from the cover. Careful not to rip any pages, Janet opened the book and began to read. 

Dear Diary, 
Today’s weather is beautiful. …

The sun shone beautifully and warmly onto the ground, but Justin couldn’t stop shivering. Three very familiar faces loomed over him. One of them, the leader, spit out a ring of smoke. “You know the drill. Give it up.” 

Extremely practiced in his movements, Justin immediately handed over his wallet without any resistance. Snatching it from him, the bullies immediately started counting the cash inside and scoffed when they only scraped out five dollars. 

“Five bucks? That’s all you have? Are you playing with us?” 

Justin kept his head down, knowing that if he lifted it, he would get some nonsense on how no one was worthy of looking straight at them and a sucker punch to the gut. 

“Hey! Are you listening!” one bully yelled out rhetorically, stirring Justin from his thoughts. 

“Yes,” Justin deadpanned. 

“Hm? I don’t really believe you, but I’m in a good mood, so I’ll let it go for today.” 

Justin allowed himself to feel the slightest bit of hope that he would not go back sopping wet or with a new bruise. His hopes were dashed. 

“Oh my god!” Anna cried once she saw Justin. She ran up to where he was wringing out his shirt near a tree in the park where they were supposed to meet up. “It was them again, right? I’m so sorry!” she burst into tears. 

Justin smiled helplessly and gently patted her back, soothing her. “It’s just a little water, Anna. There’s no need to cry over this much.” 

Anna sniffled. “But … but … the reason you’re in this situation is because of me … ” 

Justin shook his head and ruffled his girlfriend’s hair. “It’s not your fault. It’s theirs.” He cupped her cheeks so that she was looking straight at him. “I have never regretted what I did. I will always make the choice to save you again, understand?” 

Anna nodded. Justin gently wiped her tears away. “Now, we finally get to see each other, so can we not talk about depressing people?” 

Anna laughed. “Sure!” As the two chatted, they enjoyed the sun beaming down on them, drying Justin’s clothing that he spread out on a tree branch to dry; the gentle wind tousled their hair and whispered past their ears; and the chatter of the birds that streaked the sky in all sorts of colors. Finally, the two had to part and they left with the hope of seeing each other the next day. If only Justin had seen the determination in Anna’s eyes, the following day would have possibly turned out quite differently. 

Justin made his way home, in no hurry at all. There was no point, after all; it’s not like there was going to be someone waiting for him anyways. Justin unlocked the door and turned on the lights. The dark, cold house slowly brightened up, but there wasn’t another soul in sight. As expected, Justin found a note on the dining table to heat up the food in the fridge as both of his parents were going to be out. He dropped his bookbag onto the ground and trudged up the stairs, flopping onto his bed. He closed his eyes as dark thoughts clouded his mind. Thoughts of how he was so insignificant within this world; how the world wouldn’t even change if he wasn’t here. The waning moon shined in through the window, illuminating his face. Although tired, Justin just couldn’t fall asleep. An uncomfortable feeling nagged at him constantly, but he didn’t know what it signified. Finally, he managed to fall asleep. 

When Justin woke up the next morning, the uncomfortable feeling still hadn’t gone away; in fact, it had gotten even stronger. Frowning, Justin attempted to figure out where the feeling was originating from to no avail. He continued pondering the problem as he walked to school. He still hadn’t gotten the answer when he arrived. However, his intuition was telling him he was just about to find out. 

As he walked in, the first thing he noticed were the many police cars and officers. A huddle of students were crowded around one area and Justin went over to join them. The crowd parted once they saw him and Justin walked on, confused. When he finally reached the yellow tape, all the blood drained from his face. 

Why? He screamed in his consciousness. Why?! Why! In front of him laid a puddle of blood flowing out from its owner. Blood soaked the light brown hair dark red and the unique scent of iron filled the air. Trembling and breathing shakily, Justin took a step backward in shock, stumbling and falling backwards onto the ground before the world around him turned black.

When Justin woke up, he was in the nurse’s office. Justin stayed silent despite the copious amount of questions he had running through his mind.. His fingers were trembling and his entire body felt cold. 

“Are you okay?” Justin jolted at the nurse’s words. He never even realized there was someone else in the room. “Wha ― what happened to Anna?!” He tried to sit up but fell back down due to a wave of dizziness. 

“Don’t sit up. You fainted from fatigue and excess stress,” the nurse told him. “You can either continue to rest here or have your parents pick you up.” 

“What happened to Anna?” Justin repeated. 

The nurse looked at him. “According to police investigations, she jumped off the roof. The death was ruled a suicide.” 

Justin’s vision turned blood red. “That’s impossible!” he yelled. “Anna would never do that!” 

“There were eyewitness testimonies of what happened.” 

Justin felt his stomach drop. “Who?” he demanded. 

“Pardon?” the nurse asked. 

“Who were the witnesses?” he asked, his voice at absolute zero. 

The nurse gave the three names that made Justin freeze inside, his soul leaving him. They were the names  of the bullies that had been bothering him and Anna. As soon as Justin heard the names, he knew that there was more to the story. He immediately got off the bed, ignoring his  lightheadedness he was, and frantically ran out the door. 

“Hey, wait! You should be resting!” The nurse called out after him, but her voice fell on deaf ears. 

Justin rushed through the hallways, ignoring the weird looks he got from those around him. The roof was closed for investigations, but Justin already knew where the bullies would be. He raced to the alleyway where he was usually extorted money from, and sure enough, three familiar faces were standing there, smoking. If it were any other day, Justin would be scared out of his mind, but his anger took over his rationality. 

“What did you do to her?!” he screamed at the bullies as he grabbed one of them and shook them wildly. 

The bully shook at Justin, trying to shake him off. “Who the heck are you talking about?” 

Justin directly punched the bully. “I. Said. What. Did. You. Do. To. Anna.” Every one of his words were enunciated slowly. It was by no means loud, but it penetrated the ears and sent shivers down the bullies’ spines. Then they got mad that they got scared by this puny boy. 

“Ha!” the leader laughed. “She came to us! Told us to stop bothering you, or else! So we asked her what ‘what else’ entailed and she couldn’t answer!” 

All of the bullies burst out in mocking laughter. “It was so hilarious! She was shaking, but was pretending to be brave; it was so entertaining!” 

The three continued their raucous laughter, leaving one fuming Justin to try to punch them, but was easily thrown into the wall. His back hit the wall and he coughed up a mouthful of blood. 

“So then, she stood on the ledge and said that if we didn’t stop, she would jump off. What a joke! Like she would have the guts to do that. So we gave her a little ‘push.’” The bullies started cracking up again. “There were suspicions, but our parents threatened to retract their investment or whatnot, and the school doesn’t want a bad reputation, so it’s being declared an accident. The investigation is merely a formality. It’s a win-win situation.” 

The leader stepped on Justin’s hand, causing him to let out a scream. “So don’t go telling anyone about this, okay?” Justin’s head was then shoved into the ground as the bullies all sauntered away. 

Justin picked himself up, shaking. But it wasn’t from fear, but anger. They pushed her off the roof, yet they felt no remorse for what they did at all. Justin forced himself to take several deep breaths and the raging anger soon calmed down into a chill-biting cold. 

Justin smiled sinisterly. They say that the new moon represents new beginnings. So what would happen if their ‘new beginning’ was taken away on one? Justin pulled out his phone and calculated when the next new moon was going to be. Next week, huh? I’d better move fast, then. 

Justin waited a few days for the investigation to ‘wrap up’ before climbing up to the newly reopened roof. He looked around before seeing a security camera. He checked around the area and measured the angles, seeing where the best spot to stage his play would be. He jumped when he heard the door open. He looked over to see Matthew. “Oh, it’s only you.” He continued his work, completely ignoring the new person on the roof. 

“What are you doing?” Matthew asked. Justin didn’t respond. 

“I asked, what are you doing?!” Matthew repeated more forcefully. 

Justin simply turned his head to glare at him before continuing to fiddle with the camera. 

Matthew sighed and combed his hand through his hair. “I’m sorry,” he apologized. “I’m just on edge after … you know.” 

Justin’s hands shook slightly after Matthew’s words, but he forced down his stormy emotions. Matthew sighed again. “How are you doing?” 

Justin opened his mouth, his voice hoarse from crying and many sleepless nights. “What do you think?” 

Matthew opened his mouth to speak, but nothing would come out. After all, however bad Matthew was doing, Justin had to be doing several times worse. Justin jumped down from where he was standing and started to the door. As he left, he heard Matthew’s voice behind him. “Don’t do anything stupid,” his best friend said, completely ignored by the person it was intended for. 

During the next couple of days, as Justin spread rumors on how Anna actually died, there were small, momentary bouts of clarity within his mind ― on days that were just like the one where he last saw Anna. The wind whipping her hair around, her cheerful laughter that glowed in the sun, curved eyes, and bright smile were etched into his mind, becoming the things that brought his mental state back from collapse. However, it was those same things that drove him right back to the edge: how he couldn’t see those things ever again, and it was all their fault. 

Finally, six days later, on the night of the full moon, Justin’s plan was completed. He was standing on the rooftop, leaning on an old railguard, in front of the camera, when the door banged open, revealing three very angry faces. 

“Hello,” Justin greeted, his calm a mere mask to his turbulent emotions. 

“How dare you!” the leader snarled. 

Justin laughed mockingly. “What did you expect? That I was going to keep my mouth shut? How stupid are you?! You guys told me what you did to Anna ― my girlfriend ― and you thought I was going to just shut up and take that?!” Justin’s emotions, that had been suppressed for so long, burst out in a torrent. “You killed her! You pushed her down the roof and then you framed it as an accident due to your oh-so-powerful parents. Then what about Anna?! Who’s going to pay for what you’ve done?!” Justin screamed. He panted, watching as the expressions on the bullies’ faces changed. Just one more push. He curled up his lips in a mocking smile. “It’s your fault.” 

At those three words, the bullies snapped and grabbed his collar, shaking him wildly. They slapped his cheeks. “Huh?! What did you say, you nutjob?! Your girlfriend deserved it! She had it coming! She was the one threatening to commit suicide if we wouldn’t stop bothering you! And you dare to say it’s OUR fault?!” The bullies threw Justin against the railing. 

Ignoring the disorientation as he flew through the air, as he fell towards the guardrail, Justin adjusted his position so that he fell against one specific spot that was loose, dismantling the railing and pushing himself off the edge. 

Justin smiled, satisfied, as he felt the wind whistling past his ears and the weightlessness of his back. Many people say that their lives flash before their eyes as they’re about to die, but Justin only saw Anna. She smiled helplessly at him once she saw what he had done. I’m coming to see you … Justin’s body hit the ground with a thump. 

Janet closed the diary. Her heart, at some unknown period of time, had started to race. Her hands were trembling and she squeezed her eyes shut, in an attempt to steady her shaky breathing and chills running down her spine. She had never realized … that the brother that she had hated so much, that she had resented with every fiber of her being, had gone through so much. She could feel the sadness, horror, despair, anger, and hatred tangled up within the pages of the diary. All of those feelings had been bubbling within Justin, having no place to vent, until they exploded into his revenge. 

Carefully making her way down the ladder, Janet found Matthew waiting for her. “What happened?” Janet heard herself asking. 

Matthew’s voice was quiet as he replied, “After he jumped, they were convicted of manslaughter. Normally, they wouldn’t have been, but the surveillance camera caught everything, and the angle didn’t show anything about Justin pushing the rail.” 

“I see.” Janet didn’t meet his eyes as she walked to the door; she just couldn’t. She had resented Justin, but now that she understood him, she felt guilty for hating him. Within her head that was still a mess, there was only one coherent thought that could form. Matthew pulled on her arm. “Where are you going?” 

“To set things straight.” 

“You can’t.” 

“Why not? As much as I sympathize with him, Justin still did something wrong. People who shouldn’t be in jail ended up ― “ 

“But they should,” Matthew interrupted. “They never paid for what they did to Anna, so Justin made them, even if they went to jail for killing the wrong person.” 

Janet opened her mouth to speak, but Matthew cut her off again. “Besides, for Justin, getting revenge was, honestly, secondary. You should’ve seen it as well. Within the anger and hatred, held a resolve ― a wish ― to die. Framing them was a fortunate byproduct.” 

Janet’s fists trembled, but she still stubbornly refused to move. 

“I knew,” Matthew finally whispered. “I knew what he was planning.” His voice and his arms trembled. “I knew that he was planning on killing himself and blaming it on them, but I couldn’t stop him. I was his closest friend, but he didn’t say anything to me!” 

Tears fell from his eyes, and his words came out in slurred, but Matthew continued speaking. “I! I couldn’t help him at all!” He let go of Janet’s arm as he collapsed on the floor. 

Janet squatted down next to him and patted his back. “Justin didn’t write anything incriminating, you know. He never explicitly wrote anything about his plan; just about his hatred towards them.” 

Janet looked at Matthew in shock. “You’ve read the diary too?” 

Matthew nodded. “Your mom and dad, too. It was because they read it that they knew that their distance from Justin was another factor in his suicide. Which is why they’re so overprotective with you now.” 

Janet looked down at the ground in shame. 

“That’s why,” Matthew continued. “Talk to them. Tell them about your thoughts, about how you need your own space. If you don’t, you’ll just keep pushing them away and isolating yourself. You’ll become another Justin.”

Janet sniffled, but she finally decided to go back to eat dinner with her parents. 

Later, she went to see them when they were released from prison. They were big, with a much more terrifying aura than what Justin had described in his diary. 

Janet quietly walked past them, hoping to overhear parts of their conversation. What she heard astounded her. 

“Those brats!” one of them cursed. “If it weren’t for them, our lives would be so much better!” 

“Exactly!” another echoed. “If I see them, I’ll kill them!”
 
“HAHA!” a third laughed. “They’re already dead, though!” At his words, the three of them started cackling boisterously, not caring for the annoyed stares others were sending them. 

Janet clenched her fists as she listened to their conversation that showed absolutely no remorse for what they had done. She stared holes into their backs, flames burning in her dark eyes. Janet opened her phone. The weather app said that tonight was going to be a new moon. 

The End.

Clarity

“You are being removed from your position.” 

Leo had been informed of this decision through an email. An email! No one had the courtesy to tell him of this decision in person, though that was due to Leo’s less than amiable personality and …… unique temperament. 

Of course, since Leo was the son of the CEO, he naturally did not listen to this email, stating, “What does the rabble know?” 

He merely brushed the email off as a scam, one that someone was going to be paying for when he came into the office. Of course, as the spoiled, selfish, only son of the CEO, he naturally had zero clue as to who had sent him the email, even though they were on the board of directors and was by no means “rabble.” 

Leo ordered his limousine to be driven to the company and, as usual, arrived there without a smidgen of elegance. Roughly slamming the door and loudly chastising the driver for driving “slower than a snail,” Leo headed in, stomping boorishly. The driver contemplated driving away, leaving Leo to breathe in the exhaust gas left in his wake, but he soon decided against it. He still needed this job, after all. 

As soon as Leo came in, he brusquely walked past the front desk and was promptly stopped by the secretary. 

“What do you mean I’m not allowed in?!” Leo screamed, pointing rudely at the secretary’s nose. “I am the son of the CEO. Plebeians like you don’t understand a thing!” 

The secretary had been working here for many years and was long since used to dealing with these coddled brats of the rich. Calmly holding her ground, she replied, “I have my orders from the top. Please take it up with them.” 

“I will! Once you let me through!” 

“I am afraid you do not have an appointment,” the secretary replied.

“Heh! Like that’ll stop me!” Leo boasted. “My dad is the CEO!” He happily brandished his only weapon of his parents being important. 

The secretary gave him a blank stare. “Yes, and … ?”

Leo faltered slightly before hiding his confusion behind his arrogant facade. All his life, once others heard that his dad was the CEO of Spire Inc. and his mom was a famous career woman, they would immediately fall over themselves in an attempt to ingratiate themselves with him. Seeing someone who was so composed upon hearing of his parents’ position without a doubt came as a shock to him. 

But he quickly pulled himself back together as he threatened, “Do you know what will happen to your job if I call them right now?” 

Completely unfazed, the secretary responded, “You are welcome to call whomever you wish.” It won’t make an impact on the result though.

Scoffing, Leo dialed his dad’s number. As soon as his dad picked up, Leo immediately complained, “Dad! The secretary’s bullying me! She won’t let me in the company!” 

A gruff voice could be heard from the other end of the call. “And what business do you have at the company?” 

The voice was laced with tiredness and frustration, but Leo was too agitated to notice. “What do you mean ‘what business’? I’m a project manager!” 

“Former project manager,” his father replied. “There have been numerous complaints about your atrocious behavior in the past half a year. It was a unanimous decision by the board to have you dismissed. Have you not received the email detailing this situation to you?”

“You mean that hoax of an email? I was just coming in to fire the person who sent it!” 

“Leo! How arrogant must you be to fire one of the board of directors even though you are a measly project manager?! Not to mention, Mr. Giles is the son of the chairman and holds immense sway in the company! If you go mouthing off like that in front of him, it won’t just be your head that rolls; it’ll be mine as well! How could I have raised such a thankless son?! Thanks to you, I now have to clean up your mess! Your group is the only one that has not even done an iota of work yet, but has the largest turnover rate in the entire company! If you won’t listen to these words over an email, then I’ll tell them to you again right now: You are fired! Never step foot into this company ever again!” 

With those words, Leo’s father hung up. Stunned, Leo’s hand slowly fell down. Quickly redialing his father’s cell number, the only response Leo received was the busy tone. He immediately tried to rush inside the building, only to be stopped by security. 

“Let go of me!” Leo yelled. “I have to speak to my father!” 

The two guards quickly hauled Leo out the door while the secretary called the CEO. “He doesn’t want to see you,” the secretary informed him, a light smirk on her face. She was without a doubt enjoying handing this rascal his ass since she had never been able to talk back to him before. After all, this little kid was nothing without the backing of his parents. 

“You’re lying!” Leo cried. 

“I am not. Your father’s explicit words were ‘don’t let me hear about that ungrateful ingrate! He is no longer my son!’”

Unable to even get a word in, Leo ― face bright red in embarrassment ― decidedly sat in the waiting room, stubborn as a mule, waiting for someone to notice his plight and let him in. The security guards let him in, but kept an eye out on his behavior. 

Alas! The only attention Leo received was from idle gossipers who passed by, talking about how glad they were that a certain selfish punk was no longer in the company. Upon noticing that certain brat, half either scurried away quickly, afraid of his parents’ retaliation, or happily ridiculed him in schadenfreude, making sure that he was within earshot as they slandered him. 

“Oh, I’m so happy that that urchin is no longer here!” 

“He truly was worthless, wasn’t he?”

“His only use was being an example of what not to do for the new hires!” 

Leo turned redder and redder as he listened to their conversations, whether in anger or embarrassment, even he didn’t know. Finally unable to take it, Leo fled outside, mocking laughter trailing behind him. 

Drained, he sat on the sidewalk. The obnoxious noises of the city sounded in his ears, but he was unable to comprehend them. His mind was filled with the words of his father, the secretary, and contemptuous insults of the onlookers. 

How could I have raised such a thankless son?!

Your father doesn’t want to see you!

Never step into this company again!

I’m so happy that that urchin is no longer here!

He is no longer my son!

Tears dripped down his face as the words echoed repeatedly in his mind. The tear trails mixed with the rain as it began to fall from the sky. Had it been even an hour before, Leo would have haughtily ordered his “lackeys” to grab him an umbrella. But right now, he was too exhausted, too tired of it all, to care. Watching the cars whizz by, Leo thought about how easy it would just be to walk in front of one … 

But before he had time to further contemplate on that line of thought, an umbrella was placed over his head. Leo shivered slightly as he finally felt the chill of sitting outside in the rain without the proper garments to protect himself from it. Looking up, Leo focused on the unfamiliar face in front of him. “Who ― ?”

“You’re Leo, right?” the girl interrupted. 

In his listless state, Leo didn’t have the brain power to contemplate how this absolute stranger even knew him before nodding his head. 

“I was right!” the girl cried excitedly. “I thought I saw someone like you sitting on the sidewalk in the rain, but who would’ve thought it actually was you?” 

Leo shook his head in an attempt to clear his lackadaisical mindset. With his mind muddled, he unconsciously reverted to the way that he spoke in his mind. “Excuse me, but you are … ?”

The girl blushed slightly. “Oh, do you not remember me? I’m Claire.”

Leo stared at the girl for a while, trying to place the name to a face. “Claire … ?” His eyebrows unconsciously furrowed. “Claire … Claire … Claire … ! You mean the Claire that I used to play with when I was younger?!” 

Claire giggled. “Unless you know another Claire.” 

Leo laughed happily, the first genuine positive emotion that he had felt all day. “Sorry for not recognizing you. It’s just … It’s been so long since we’ve met … ”

Claire laughed. “You don’t have to be so polite; we’re still considered childhood friends even if I did go abroad for like a decade to broaden my horizons.” The girl tilted her head, finally realizing that something was off. “Why were you sitting in the rain like this? I mean, back then, if you had so much as played in the mud, there would be a line of servants at your beck and call to clean you up.” 

Leo’s expression darkened. Claire immediately noticed the change in expression and quickly amended, “You don’t have to tell me if you don’t want to.” 

Leo shook his head. The memories that had brought him much anguish had suddenly become less painful once talking about it with Claire. She had always had that ability, making it much easier to share anything with her. 

“I got disowned,” he said, shrugging his shoulders. 

His tone was so lighthearted that it took a few seconds for Claire to fully comprehend the meaning of his words. “Hah?!” 

She immediately grabbed onto his lapels and shook him harshly. “What do you mean by that?!” 

“Wah!” was Leo’s response, and Claire immediately let go. 

“S-Sorry,” she said sheepishly.

“It’s fine,” Leo managed to get out. “Just kind of dizzy.” He took a deep breath before recounting the events that had just happened. 

“Wow,” was Claire’s response. 


“‘Wow’ in a good way or bad way?” [Leo]

“‘Wow’ as in I don’t know what to say.” [Claire]

The two stayed in an awkward silence until Leo finally said, “Let’s get out of the rain first,” and quietly led Claire under the awning of a nearby restaurant. 

Leo shook the raindrops off of himself as Claire exclaimed, “Okay, I know what to say now!”

Leo laughed at her random outburst. “Okay, okay, please tell me all of the ways I screwed up,” he said in a joking tone. 

“Well, first of all, you were being a prick,” Claire said bluntly. 

Metaphorical daggers stabbed into him and he coughed blood. “Jeez, you didn’t have to start like that … ”

“Well then, how would you like me to start?” [Claire]

“How about, ‘I know you were having a tough time, so here are some things we can work on’?” [Leo]

“Okay; I know you were having a tough time, so the first thing you should work on is to stop being a prick.” [Claire]

Leo facepalmed. “That’s not much better … ”

Claire smiled sweetly. “I started it the way you wanted me to~”

Leo blew his bangs out of his face. “Why don’t you just not say anything then?” 

“Oh, Leo can dish it, but he can’t take it? How sad~” Claire sang happily. 

Leo’s eyebrow twitched. “Alright; I’ll stop interrupting … ”

Claire laughed again, and her tone finally turned serious. “Why don’t you think about it for a second? Your own personality, how would you define it?” 

Leo blinked a couple of times in succession as he processed her question. He had been expecting to be chewed out again, so having a question thrown at him threw him for a loop. “My personality, huh … ” he muttered quietly. 

“ … Confident,” he said finally. “Confident, proud, self-assured, perfectionist … That’s all I got for now.” 

“And translated, that turns into arrogant, cocky, self-important, and judgmental. Do you see the problem?” [Claire]

Leo tilted his head. “I don’t follow.” 

Claire sighed. “The translated terms of your adjectives are simply the more negative sides of your personality. Confidence is great, but taken too far is simply arrogance. The same goes for pride and self-assuredness. As for being a perfectionist, you demand perfection not only from yourself, but also your subordinates. And the way you do so comes off as being rude since others don’t have the same measure of ‘perfect’ that you do.” 

What Claire didn’t bother mentioning was that, judging from his story, his subordinates were probably too proud to work under a child that was at least three decades younger than them, and one that had probably been spoiled since birth. They had probably done a less than commendable job, while justifying it to themselves that it was due to Leo’s personality problems. But that was something that wasn’t very important now. After all, she was supposed to be helping Leo fix his shortcomings and not the ones of his former subordinates. 

And considering how Leo still didn’t seem to get it, he was going to be needing a lot of help. 

“Ugyah!” Claire yelped in frustration. 

“Whoa! You scared me.” [Leo]

“How do you still not understand?! Are you a blockhead or just plain slow?! I practically spelled it out for you!” [Claire]

“Then why don’t you tell me instead of beating around the bush?!” [Leo]

“There’s no point in it then!” [Claire]

The two panted, tired of yelling at each other. Then one of them laughed and the other soon followed, raucous laughter filling the streets. Passersby gave them curious glances but soon hurried on with their own lives. 

“Hehe, we used to argue just like this when we were kids, right?” Claire snickered. 

Leo smirked. “Yeah. Gave the servants an earful when they couldn’t calm us down.” He smiled quietly. “I haven’t felt this free in a long time … ”

The wind quietly blew past his ears, gently tousling his light brown hair. Clear green eyes stared up at the sky as the clouds cleared to let the sunshine in. Claire’s own blue eyes fell on Leo. After their spat, the malice in his body just seemed to lift away. His current figure was reminiscent of their childhood, where they would say … and do anything they wanted, before the reality of life set in, before they learned of society’s cruelties, before … before everything happened. 

The quiet silence around them and the gentle rays of the sun as it peeked out into the sky helped the little cogs in Leo’s brain turn. His eyes dimmed as he reflected. 

“I see what you mean,” he whispered quietly after his introspection. His voice was low, but Claire could hear every word of what he said. “I wouldn’t want to work under someone like me either.” A slight melancholy permeated his voice, constricting his throat as he murmured, “But I didn’t want this either … I was just following them … ”

It was Claire’s turn to be confused. But Leo was in no mood to answer her doubts. He crouched down, burying his face in his arms, looking strangely akin to a little kid who just got scolded by his parents. 

“Are you okay?” Claire asked. 

Leo hesitated slightly, yet still chose to stubbornly nod his head, even though his entire being was screaming out how he wasn’t okay right now. Quiet sniffling could be heard as Leo tried to stifle his sobs. He didn’t want to see anyone right now. Not when he was feeling as if the world under his feet was coming crashing down. 

Claire didn’t speak. She spared him the embarrassment by not pointing out the obvious. She let him cry to himself to vent his emotions. She didn’t interrupt, nor hurry him up. She simply stayed by his side while he sorted out his feelings. A few minutes later, a quiet voice was heard. 

“Ever since I was little, my parents always told me to hold myself up as the son of Spire Inc.’s CEO. I was told to not mingle with commoners, and that if I was to act humble, everyone would walk over me. So when I started acting as a narcissistic prince, my parents acknowledged that my current attitude was the right one. But now you’re saying that it was wrong? That my perceptions were misguided? Then what the hell have I been doing with the past seventeen years of my life?!” Leo’s despairing voice shook. “Ha … haha … hahahahaha! My entire life! Was a lie! Hahaha!” 

Claire could tell that Leo’s mind was one step away from falling into a direction that he would never be able to recover from. “Not everything was a lie,” she quickly amended. “Nothing you’ve done is a waste of time.” 

Leo slowly looked up at her. The edges of his eyes were red and a thin sheen of tears gathered in his eyes. 

Claire gave him the brightest smile she could muster. “Everything you’ve done in life is meaningful. Every experience you’ve had makes you who you are. Failures are the makers of success.” 

Leo scoffed. “That’s just what people tell failures so that they don’t feel like losers.” 

Claire used every might of willpower she could muster to stop herself from retorting, ‘That comment of yours perfectly describes your situation right now!’ in favor of something a little more constructive. “Someone once told me, ‘The only difference between a master and a novice is that the master has failed more times than the novice has tried.’ So how are you going to become a master if you don’t fail? You don’t really think that no one has failed before, right?” Her voice held a slightly provocative tone, in hopes that Leo would perk up in the ensuing argument. 

Leo didn’t bite. “I haven’t failed.” After all, his parents were always there to pick up his mess. 

Claire rolled her eyes. “Then what does this constitute as?” she asked, referring to the entire situation that landed him in this mess in the first place. 

“ … I don’t know.” [Leo]

Claire sighed in exasperation. “Then, since you don’t have any experience in cleaning up after your failures, then you can start right now.” She held out her hand to Leo. “I’m going to be starting a company, so you can join as my project manager.” 

“Huh?” [Leo]

“What? It’s what I came back for anyways. And since you don’t have a job or family to rely on, then you can just accept my offer.” [Claire]

“Wh … Why?” Leo asked, confusion leaking from his voice. 

“Hmm … think of it as an investment!” Claire replied. She knew that Leo’s pride would never accept her giving him charity, so she simply had to phrase it differently. “I’ll help you now, so you can elevate my company to bigger heights later!” 

Leo stared at her incomprehensibly. But he just couldn’t turn down the goodwill sparkling in his childhood friend’s eyes. “Fine,” he said finally, taking her hand as she pulled him up. It wasn’t like he had anything to do anyways. 

“But we’re totally fixing your personality first,” Claire said, quickly draining the positive emotions from the conversation. 

“What do you mean by that?!” Leo yelled in anger. 

“Exactly what I said! If you keep that attitude while working in my company, then you’ll scare off all my employees!” [Claire]

“Huh?! Couldn’t you have phrased that better?!” [Leo]

“I’m talking to you so there’s no need for that!” [Claire]

The two continued to bicker loudly, ignoring all the weird looks they received from their surroundings, before the restaurant owner came out and chastised them for disrupting their business. 


~~~

In the far, far future, a certain company took the business world by storm. They quickly rose the ranks and were even said to be a worthy competitor for the Spire Inc.. Rumor has it that the main contributor to their success was the disowned son of Spire Inc.’s CEO. But those rumors were never confirmed. 

The only rumor that had been confirmed was that the founder of the company and the main project manager argued like cats and dogs and that it continued, even on the day of their wedding.

The End.
